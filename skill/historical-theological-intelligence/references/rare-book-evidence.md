# Rare-Book Evidence

Rare-book mode inventories only what the image can support: format clues, page furniture, foliation or pagination, signatures, catchwords, running heads, ornaments, type changes, manuscript additions, damage, binding intrusion, and photographic artifacts.

When reader interventions are requested, scan the full supplied page for textual annotations and non-textual marks. Describe marks by location and legible content. Use [image processing](image-processing.md) for explicit questions such as the reading of an upper-margin word or the separation of a marginal mark from printed furniture. Plan only the regions needed for those questions; one question may need multiple regions and separate questions may need separate regions. Every view derives directly from the original. Stop when a question resolves, or close it UNRESOLVED after its finite planned effort. A non-textual mark inventory does not establish that no textual reader-note exists. Unresolved writing remains unresolved and supplies no annotator or date.

Keep separate: signature; catchword; folio or page number; recto/verso; quire position; edition; exemplar; provenance. Do not infer paper stock, ink composition, binding date, hand date, ownership, reader or annotator identity, number of historical hands, provenance, institutional ownership, acquisition history, or printer from visual plausibility. Describe marginalia by location, extent, language or script when readable, apparent relation to text, and confidence. `No securely visible annotation` is preferable to inventing one.

Use `physical_observation` for what the supplied image shows: for example, brown or darker writing, writing that appears handwritten, visibly distinct writing styles, an upper-margin annotation, or a date-like numeral sequence. These are observations, not object history. A sequence that appears to read `1693` may be reported as a visible sequence, but it is not thereby the date of the annotation. Use `physical_history` only for ownership, reader/annotator or named-hand identity, dating, ink composition, provenance, institutional ownership, binding, acquisition, or attribution of a marginalium to a historical person. Such a claim needs independently registered documentary, catalog, material-analysis, or verified structural evidence. Page appearance alone cannot satisfy it. Run the centralized `pre-output` audit before final physical-history output.

Use typed physical-book claims. `signature_face` requires the visible signature plus an established signature convention. `catchword_continuity` requires the catchword itself. `quire_size`, `gathering_structure`, `gathering_formula`, `conjugacy`, and `internal_quire_pagination` each require independent physical or verified structural evidence. Never derive any of them from a signature, a catchword, or generic early-modern gathering practice alone. If the gathering is not independently evidenced, state that it remains unknown.

For order reconstruction, state each linkage and its evidence. Physical sequence remains unresolved when catchwords, signatures, and textual continuity conflict.
